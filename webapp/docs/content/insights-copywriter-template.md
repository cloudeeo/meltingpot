# Insights — copywriter brief & template

This is a single, self-contained brief for one Executive Founders
Insights piece. It contains everything you need: the rules of the
voice, the structure of the article, the word-count targets, and a
template to fill in. A worked example is at the bottom for reference.

If you only read one sentence: **400 words, five beats, no jargon, end
with a "if you recognise this, get in touch" hook**.

---

## What to write

A short post for the Insights section of `executivefounders.com`,
based on a single anonymised real engagement Executive Founders worked
on. Audience: C-suite, board members and senior operators in Swiss
and European mid-cap and large organisations. They are time-poor and
allergic to vague management language.

---

## Voice

**Tone**
- Measured, slightly formal Swiss professional register.
- Confident without being self-promoting.
- Diagnostic, not enthusiastic.

**Person**
- Use "we" sparingly, and only in the intervention paragraph.
  ("We rebuilt the steering cadence" — yes. "We help organisations
  realise their potential" — no.)
- The post is about the **client's situation**, not about us.

**Sentences and paragraphs**
- Short paragraphs. 4–6 paragraphs total in the body.
- Single-sentence paragraphs are allowed and encouraged for emphasis.
- Em-dashes are a tool — use them; don't carpet-bomb. ~3 in a post.

---

## Forbidden words and phrases

These never appear in an Executive Founders Insight. If you find
yourself reaching for one, the sentence needs to be rewritten:

> synergy · leverage (as a verb) · best-in-class · world-class ·
> robust · seamless · holistic · ecosystem · DNA · journey ·
> north star · double down · circle back · actionable insights ·
> take it to the next level

Two phrases to avoid because they signal vagueness:

> "we help organisations …" (passive — say what we **did**)
> "in today's fast-paced business environment" (cliché preamble)

## Structure — five beats, in this order

Every Insights post follows this exact structure. Beat 5 is one
sentence, not a paragraph.

### Beat 1 — Hook (1–2 sentences)

Drop the reader into a specific situation. Name the sector, the
scale, and one observable symptom. The reader should be able to
picture the organisation.

*Example:*
> "A fully-remote consultancy of about thirty specialists,
> distributed across eight European countries, came to us with what
> they thought was a capacity-management problem: the CEO never knew,
> at month-end, whether the workforce was actually billing what the
> plan said it should."

### Beat 2 — Tension (1–2 paragraphs)

What was breaking. Concrete symptoms, not abstract diagnoses. The
reader should think *I have seen this*.

*Example:*
> "On paper, everyone was busy. In practice, no one — including the
> managers — could say what their colleagues were working on by
> Wednesday. Client quotes were leaving the firm signed by whoever
> had picked up the lead, in whatever shape that person preferred,
> with no record of which ones had been accepted."

### Beat 3 — Intervention (1–2 paragraphs)

What Executive Founders actually did, operationally. Verbs, not
nouns. "We rebuilt the steering cadence" beats "we provided
strategic guidance".

At least one verb in this beat should be an active operational
change: *rebuilt, restructured, re-anchored, untangled, re-granted,
surfaced, retired, collapsed, separated, centralised, converted*.

*Example:*
> "We did three things, none of them clever. First, we drew the org
> as it actually was — five small teams of three to six experts —
> and made those leaders the single point of contact for every
> quote, every client request, every decision."

### Beat 4 — Outcome (1 short paragraph)

Measurable change, OR an unambiguous qualitative shift. Never "they
were happy". One sentence in this beat should be quantitative or
unambiguously qualitative.

*Example:*
> "Within a quarter the CEO knew, on a Monday morning, what the
> company would bill that month. Quote acceptance went up because
> the proposals were more consistent. Several time-and-materials
> relationships converted to FTE engagements that extended the
> average engagement length from a handful of months to over a
> year."

### Beat 5 — Closing insight (1 sentence)

The broader pattern. Stands alone as the last paragraph of the body.
Drawn from the brief's "Key insight" field.

*Example:*
> "In expert-led firms, 'we're all busy' is rarely the answer to
> 'is the work flowing'; lightweight structure is what turns
> individual motion into collective billable progress."

## Anonymisation

Posts describe real engagements but never identify the client.
**Allowed signals:**

- Sector (e.g. "a Series B fintech", "a municipal administration",
  "a fully-remote consultancy").
- Approximate scale (e.g. "about thirty specialists", "180
  engineers", "40,000 residents").
- Broad geography (e.g. "in eight European countries", "in western
  Switzerland", "in DACH").

**Not allowed:**

- Company name.
- City or specific street.
- Names of people.
- Any combination that would let a competitor identify the client.

| Field | Budget |
|---|---|
| Body (total) | **300–420 words.** Strict ceiling at 450. |
| Title | 6–10 words. No colons unless the colon earns it. |
| Subtitle (lede) | 20–32 words, complete sentence(s), no ellipsis. |
| Tags | 2–3, chosen from the list below. |
| CTA hook | 1 sentence, 20–35 words. |

**Canonical tag list** (pick 2–3):

`ai-governance`, `change-management`, `communication`, `decision-rights`,
`governance`, `m&a`, `operating-model`, `pmo`, `scaling`, `strategy`,
`transformation`

## CTA hook — what it is

The Insights section ends every post with a small box: a single
sentence inviting the reader to get in touch *if the symptom in the
post matches their own situation*.

**Format:** *"If [recognisable symptom], [understated invitation]."*

**Examples:**

- "If month-end results keep surprising you in an expert-led firm,
  the missing piece is rarely capacity — it's visibility."
- "If your administration is asking the same people to organise more
  events with the same headcount, the missing piece is rarely
  capacity — it's a coordinator."

The hook is **not** in the body. It's a separate field below. Don't
write a generic "contact us" — the hook earns the click by naming
the symptom.

## A worked example

Below is one published Insights post in the final shape. Use it as a
reference for tone, length, and beat balance.

---

```
TITLE:
When everyone is busy and nobody can tell


SUBTITLE / LEDE:
A fully-remote consultancy with ~30 specialists across eight European
countries thought it had a capacity-management problem. The real issue
was that nobody could see anyone else's work.


LOCALE:
en


TAGS:
operating-model, communication, decision-rights


BODY:

A fully-remote consultancy of about thirty specialists, distributed
across eight European countries, came to us with what they thought
was a capacity-management problem: the CEO never knew, at month-end,
whether the workforce was actually billing what the plan said it
should.

On paper, everyone was busy. In practice, no one — including the
managers — could say what their colleagues were working on by
Wednesday. Client quotes were leaving the firm signed by whoever had
picked up the lead, in whatever shape that person preferred, with no
record of which ones had been accepted. The same expert might be
sketching a proposal for one client and burning days on an
undocumented favour for another, and the only person tracking it was
the expert.

The pattern is familiar in expert-led firms: a generation of senior
people who were independent before they joined, still operating like
independents, with a CEO trying to read the month-end P&L for clues.

We did three things, none of them clever. First, we drew the org as
it actually was — five small teams of three to six experts, each
with a natural leader the others already turned to — and made those
leaders the single point of contact for every quote, every client
request, every decision. If a team member fielded a request directly,
they brought it to their leader before answering.

Second, we placed a single COO-shaped role between the CEO and the
team leaders, running a weekly half-hour portfolio review: status,
capacity, blockers, decisions, on one page, every Monday. Third, we
centralised quoting under that role — one template, one acceptance
log, one person accountable for follow-up. Where the engagement made
sense, we converted client work from time-and-materials to
fixed-FTE arrangements; clients accepted the predictability and
signed for longer terms.

Within a quarter the CEO knew, on a Monday morning, what the company
would bill that month. Quote acceptance went up because the
proposals were more consistent and the follow-up was no longer ad
hoc. Client complaints fell, and several time-and-materials
relationships converted to FTE engagements that extended the average
engagement length from a handful of months to over a year.

In expert-led firms, "we're all busy" is rarely the answer to "is
the work flowing"; lightweight structure is what turns individual
motion into collective billable progress.


CTA HOOK:
If month-end results keep surprising you in an expert-led firm, the
missing piece is rarely capacity — it's visibility.


IMAGE BRIEF:
A single executive standing at a floor-to-ceiling window of a quiet
office at dusk, holding a coffee, viewed from inside the room. No
people visible at desks. Composed, contemplative.
```

Body length: 377 words. Five beats, in order. Two em-dashes. No "we"
outside Beat 3. Quantitative outcome ("from a handful of months to
over a year"). CTA hook follows the *if … the missing piece is …*
pattern.

## How to send the draft

Send the filled-in template as a **Markdown (.md) file attached to an
email** to `drafts@executivefounders.com`. Optional: attach one image
(JPG/PNG/WebP). The system picks up the email automatically, parses
the fields, and queues the draft for editorial review. Only mail from
a pre-approved sender address is accepted; anything else is silently
discarded.

Questions or clarifications on a specific brief: just reply to the
email with the assignment.
